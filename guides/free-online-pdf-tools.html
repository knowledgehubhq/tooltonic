Free Online PDF Tools Worth Knowing

By the ToolTonic editorial team · Published September 27, 2026

PDFs are the universal currency of documents: contracts, resumes, forms, ebooks, scanned paperwork. But the moment you need to merge two PDFs, shrink an oversized file, or pull pages out of one, most people discover their computer has no obvious way to do it. These free online tools fill that gap, and knowing which one to reach for saves real time.

What these tools actually do

The core jobs people need are remarkably consistent: combine several PDFs into one, split one PDF into pieces, compress a PDF that is too big to email, convert a PDF into an editable Word document or into images, rotate or reorder pages, add page numbers, and fill in or sign a form. Every tool below handles most of that list directly in the browser.

iLovePDF — the best all-rounder

iLovePDF is the most polished free option and the one to try first. The workflow is the same for every task: pick the tool (merge, split, compress, convert), drag your files in, arrange them if needed, and download the result. Merging is genuinely pleasant — you can drag thumbnails to reorder pages across documents before combining. Compression offers three levels, and the "recommended" setting typically cuts file size by 60 to 80 percent with no visible difference on screen.

The free tier is generous but not unlimited: tasks are capped per hour, and some conversions add a small watermark or limit batch sizes. For occasional use — the resume tweak, the merged application packet — you will never hit the wall. Heavy daily users will eventually be nudged toward the paid plan, which is fair enough for a tool this good.

PDF24 Tools — the privacy-friendly pick

PDF24 takes a different approach: it also offers a free desktop application for Windows that does everything offline. If you are handling anything sensitive — tax documents, medical forms, legal contracts — the desktop version is the better choice because your files never touch a server. The online version at tools.pdf24.org covers the same tasks as iLovePDF with no account required and, unusually, no obvious usage caps on most tools.

The interface is less slick than iLovePDF's, and conversions can be slightly slower, but for a completely free tool with an offline option, it is hard to beat.

Other solid options

  • Smallpdf has a clean, beginner-friendly interface and excellent compression, but the free plan is the most restrictive here — typically two tasks per day. Fine for emergencies, frustrating as a daily driver.
  • Adobe's free online tools (from the company that invented PDF) handle the basics well and the conversions are high quality, though you will need a free Adobe account for some features.
  • Sejda is a quieter alternative with a thoughtful free tier: a few tasks per hour, files up to 50 MB, and a clear statement that uploaded files are deleted after a few hours.

Step by step: the three most common jobs

Merging several PDFs into one

  1. Open iLovePDF's Merge PDF tool (or PDF24's equivalent).
  2. Drag your files in, then drag the thumbnails into the order you want.
  3. Click merge, download the combined file, and spot-check the page order before sending it anywhere.

Shrinking a PDF that is too big to email

  1. Use the Compress PDF tool and start with the recommended/medium setting.
  2. Open the result and zoom in on any images and small text. If everything looks clean, you are done.
  3. If it is still too large, try the stronger setting — but check quality again, because heavy compression can make scanned text fuzzy.

Converting a PDF to an editable Word document

  1. Use the PDF-to-Word converter and upload your file.
  2. Download the .docx and expect to fix formatting: tables, columns, and headers often need manual cleanup.
  3. Know the limit: converters work well on text-based PDFs but struggle with scanned pages. A scanned PDF is really just images of text — for that you need OCR (optical character recognition), which iLovePDF and PDF24 both offer as a separate tool, with mixed accuracy on messy scans.

The privacy warning that matters

This is the most important section of this article. Online PDF tools work by uploading your document to someone else's server, processing it there, and giving you the result. Reputable services delete files after a few hours and say so in their privacy policies. But you are still trusting a stranger's infrastructure with your content.

Do not upload sensitive documents to online PDF tools. Tax returns, medical records, legal contracts, anything with ID numbers or financial details — process those with offline software instead. PDF24's free desktop app, LibreOffice Draw (free, open source), or the built-in tools on your OS handle most jobs without anything leaving your machine. On a Mac, Preview can merge, reorder, and annotate PDFs natively. On Windows 11, the free Microsoft Print to PDF plus a desktop tool covers the basics.

For non-sensitive files, stick to well-known services with clear deletion policies, and avoid obscure "free PDF" sites plastered with download buttons — those are a classic source of bundled junkware and misleading ads.

Quick decision guide

  • Occasional, non-sensitive tasks: iLovePDF — fastest and easiest.
  • Sensitive documents: PDF24 desktop app or your OS's built-in tools — nothing uploaded.
  • No-limits online use: PDF24's web tools or Sejda.
  • Best compression quality: Smallpdf or iLovePDF's recommended setting.

PDF chores are one of those things that feel annoying until you know the right tool, and then they take ninety seconds. Bookmark one online service for quick jobs and install one offline tool for sensitive ones, and you will never be stuck staring at an uncooperative document again.
